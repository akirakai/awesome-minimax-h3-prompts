## 3. Drama-expression study using observable facial-action direction

- **Video:** https://x.com/AIPixLab/status/2096390421626880069/video/1
- **New demonstration post:** https://x.com/AIPixLab/status/2096390421626880069
- **Prompt method and expression library:** https://x.com/AIPixLab/status/2093336682649231362
- **Published:** 2026-09-06 09:10 JST
- **Author:** AI写真ラボ / AIバーチャルカメラマン (@AIPixLab)
- **Theme:** a vertical compilation tests how closely H3 follows practical drama-performance directions across subtle and contrasting facial states.
- **Available output metadata:** 64.896 seconds, 720 × 1080 public stream, 2:3. The linked prompt study describes individual local H3 tests built around ten-second expression sequences.
- **Model evidence:** the new demonstration explicitly uses “#MiniMaxH3” and labels itself a practical prompt-share example; the linked author article says the expression-following tests were run locally with MiniMax H3 and provides the directing method and expression inventory.
- **Prompt method, accurately condensed:** Do not stop at an emotion label such as “suppressed laughter,” “sleepy,” or “commanding.” Translate it into visible, ordered changes in specific facial regions. For suppressed laughter, press the lips together, raise the cheeks, narrow the eyes, tighten the nose, and make the mouth corners visibly resist rising before they finally release. For sleepy-to-awake, describe the transition itself rather than two disconnected states. For a suspicious eye dart, keep the head fixed and move only the pupils. For magnetic or commanding eye contact, separately set chin angle, eyelid openness, gaze direction, jaw tension, and mouth neutrality so “commanding” does not collapse into anger. For a wink, name which eye closes, where the other eye looks, how the cheek compresses, and what the mouth corner does. Use high-contrast sequences—neutral to comic distortion, half-asleep to suddenly alert, suppressed amusement to genuine laughter—and write the timeline like actor direction: stimulus, restrained reaction, escalation, and release.
- **Reusable pattern:** decompose an expression into a facial action ledger—lips, cheeks, eyes, pupils, brows, nose, jaw, chin—and specify the order of change. Separate head motion from eye motion, and define the transition between states, not merely the start and end labels.
