## 3. Glenn Williams — diagnose connected-body failure in an edge-flow prompt

- **Author:** Glenn Williams (@glennhasabeard)
- **Published:** 2026-10-28 06:31 JST
- **Video, original post and prompt:** https://www.threads.com/@glennhasabeard/post/DfA1CNJlaeP
- **Original prompt:** “slowly oozes over the edge, stretching down in one long glossy rope”
- **Observed media:** 15.104 seconds, 640×356, near 16:9, 30 fps; locked side-on camera at table height. The author reports local ComfyUI rendering followed by Topaz upscaling to 4K.
- **Theme:** glossy slime creeps over a tabletop edge and elongates downward under gravity.
- **Observed deviation:** despite the intended single connected rope, the output splits into two strands. The author explicitly kept the take as a useful visual result rather than claiming full prompt compliance.
- **Model evidence:** the original post explicitly says “Minimax H3, open weights, generated locally” and supplies the public clip, prompt, duration, camera and render notes.

### Reusable pattern

A fixed side view and an explicit edge are effective for readable gravity-driven stretching, but “one long rope” alone does not reliably preserve connected topology. For stricter runs, add a persistent attachment contract—one body, one neck at the edge, no branching, no separation—and verify continuity rather than treating an attractive split as success.
