### Prompt, accurately summarized

Use the first frame as the sole visual authority. Lock A as the half-open patterned-glass front door in the left foreground and B as the slightly open dark rear door at deep center. The only legal route is A to B, then B back to A. She must close B fully with her right palm before returning, cross A's threshold, turn back, take A's black knob with her left hand and close A fully. Door material, hinge side, swing direction, light direction and relative distance never change; B moves only through real contact. The camera remains on one side of the corridor axis.

At 0–2.6 seconds, a fast same-side move follows her toward B and lands on a hard stop. At 2.6–7.3 seconds, she shortens her steps, lowers her center, braces the frame with her left hand and cushions B with her right palm while whispering “Easy.” The shot must hold long enough to prove the gap has fully closed; one paint chip splits into a few flakes and stops. At 7.3–11.5 seconds, her eyes snap toward A before her body turns, and a whip pan connects to a backward follow shot with closed B still visible behind her. At 11.5–15 seconds, she exits through A, brakes, controls urgent breathing, slowly closes A and keeps her palm on the door until the final tremor has settled.

Keep the threat entirely offscreen and express it only through gaze, breath, suppressed foot noise, hinge friction, dust and careful hand control. End with the woman outside and both doors visibly closed.

### Reusable pattern

For offscreen-threat escape scenes, define a fixed spatial ledger and an irreversible order of closures. Require physical confirmation of the remote barrier before the return path begins, then use eyes-before-body turns, breath, sound suppression and small material evidence to imply danger without showing it.
