### Prompt, accurately summarized

Use the reference image as the exact first frame and sole authority for the woman, clothing, backpack, aisle, shelves, products, floor and ceiling lights. Lock her to one small support area beside the foreground shelf; keep the shelf frame-left, aisle extending right-rear and the camera on the aisle side of the axis. Define four fixed points: her support area P0, red bag position R0, yellow bag position Y0 and the blue comparison bag B0 on the shelf.

The right hand owns the red bag and the left hand owns the yellow bag throughout. The blue bag never leaves B0. The red bag may touch R0 again but must never be released; the yellow bag is returned to Y0 and released only near the end. Every cut inherits hand positions, bag heights, eyelines, facing and support posture.

At 0–2 seconds, push in as she lifts red and yellow. At 2–5.5 seconds, slide about 20 cm along the shelf while she compares yellow with B0. At 5.5–7 seconds, a short 15-degree whip follows her attention back to red. At 7–11 seconds, she lowers red until its base touches R0, keeps pinching the top, rack-focuses to her face, says “Maybe this one,” then lifts red again. At 11–15 seconds, she sets yellow at Y0, rises through an explicit foot-to-knee-to-hip weight transfer, looks at red and says “No. You win.”

Keep package deformation local to finger pressure, backpack and hair secondary motion caused by body acceleration, shoe friction and support physically credible, and lighting/grade continuous. Use ventilation hum, packaging friction, floor contact, clothing, breath and the two close-miked lines; no music or subtitles.

### Reusable pattern

For compact product-choice scenes, name every candidate and fixed location, give each hand permanent ownership, and distinguish “touching down” from “releasing.” Let attention arrive in the eyes before head and camera, and make the final choice visible as an irreversible object state.
