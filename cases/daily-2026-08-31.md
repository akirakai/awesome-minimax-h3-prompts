## 1. Reusable cinematic burger-ad template

- **Video, original post, and full prompt:** https://x.com/aimikoda/status/2094279178602283013
- **Published:** 2026-08-31 13:20 JST
- **Author:** Kōda (@aimikoda)
- **Theme:** a luxury restaurant commercial that transforms one burger from macro food detail into an exploded ingredient stack, adds depth-aware kinetic typography, and ends with a rapid assembly and polished hero shot.
- **Available output metadata:** approximately 15.17 seconds; the playable X transcode is 472×270, near 16:9. The text asks for “portrait format,” but the published output is horizontal, so the actual media geometry is recorded rather than assuming a 9:16 render.
- **Model evidence:** the post explicitly begins “MiniMax H3 - Text to Video, Burger Ad,” includes the playable output, and publishes the complete generation prompt in the same post.
- **Accurate prompt summary:** Open with an extreme macro glide across a toasted bun under warm directional light, then pull back as bun, lettuce, tomato, onion, cheddar, char-grilled patty, and bottom bun separate into a perfectly aligned vertical exploded stack above a matte wooden table. Give every layer realistic weight, subtle rotation, crumbs, droplets, and steam. Alternate macro tracks, dolly pushes, slow orbits, shallow-focus passes, rack focus, and short speed ramps. Insert bold hand-lettered white words between ingredients; let the letters pass behind food, reveal through depth, stretch during transitions, and lock cleanly. Add minimal doodle strokes, a warm brown gradient, rich highlights, and deep dimensional shadows. Finish by rapidly assembling the burger, pushing in, settling the cheese and steam, and holding a centered campaign hero composition.
- **Reusable pattern:** the food item is the variable; the directing grammar stays fixed: sensory macro evidence → physically ordered exploded stack → typography embedded between layers → fast reassembly → motionless hero payoff. This makes the template portable to sandwiches, pastries, drinks, or plated dishes.

- **Published:** 2026-08-30 21:54 JST
- **Author:** John (@johnAGI168)
- **Theme:** one adult female athlete performs a pre-workout pose, two controlled hip-abduction repetitions, a Romanian deadlift, and a final back/three-quarter physique pose in a realistic phone-shot gym vlog.
- **Available output metadata:** both published outputs are approximately 15.15 seconds at 320×568, near 9:16.
- **Model evidence:** the post explicitly presents the two videos as “MiniMax H3 vs Seedance 2.5” and gives the shared prompt; the media are published in that named comparison order.
- **Accurate prompt summary:** Bind one reference image to the athlete’s face, hair, clothing, muscular but natural proportions, and skin texture; keep the gym empty of additional people. Use four timed blocks. At 0–2.4 seconds, start in a front medium-close phone shot as she composes herself and briefly flexes. At 2.4–7.2 seconds, show one complete hip-abduction cycle from a rear three-quarter low angle, a short mirror-selfie insert, and a second cycle from directly behind. At 7.2–10.8 seconds, keep her full body, barbell, feet, spine, and hip hinge visible through one technically correct Romanian deadlift. At 10.8–15 seconds, move from a rear flex to a three-quarter turn and confident look back, ending on a 0.5-second hold. Keep music, machine sounds, and barbell contact but no dialogue or captions. The negative ledger bans incorrect exercise mechanics, floating equipment, anatomical changes, sexualized angles, extra people, identity or wardrobe drift, plastic skin, text, and watermarks.
- **Reusable pattern:** when testing models with human exercise, treat biomechanics as a continuity problem. Define the joint initiating each movement, equipment contact points, the complete repetition cycle, the camera area that must remain visible for judging form, and the exact failure substitutions to reject.

## 3. Roman-atrium dinner-party I2V case study

- **Original post with all attempt videos and settings:** https://www.reddit.com/r/StableDiffusion/comments/1w30zb1/minimax_h3_case_study_the_dinner_party/
- **Published:** 2026-08-31 11:37 JST
- **Author:** DG86
- **Theme:** a static wide shot of a formal dinner party in a Roman-style atrium, used to diagnose crowd motion, camera stability, sound drift, sampler behavior, resolution, shift, caching, and Turbo-versus-base tradeoffs.
- **Available output metadata:** each attempt is 7 seconds. Tests cover 864×480 and 1344×768; hardware is an RTX 4060 Laptop GPU with 8 GB VRAM.
- **Model evidence:** the author explicitly identifies H3 I2V and the exact base checkpoint `minimax_h3_fl2va_pruned_int8_convrot`. Several published attempts run the base checkpoint at 20 steps with the Turbo LoRA disabled; accelerated comparison attempts are labeled separately.
- **Accurate prompt/workflow summary:** A reference atrium image is expanded into a well-attended dinner-party first frame. The initial H3 prompt merely names the formal dinner party, leading to frozen guests, clipping, narration, or unwanted music. The corrected prompt explicitly requires a static wide shot, visible eating, drinking, talking and mingling, a fixed camera, party ambience, and no non-diegetic score. The author compares shift 12 against 6, 864×480 against 1344×768, `res_multistep/simple` against `er_sde/beta`, 6-step Turbo against 20-step base, and EasyCache. A 20-step base run with the corrected prompt produces the desired crowd behavior but mild cache-related texture flutter; a later low-resolution Turbo run is the quickest acceptable draft.
- **Reusable pattern:** before increasing steps or resolution, name the missing behavior and lock the camera in the prompt. For crowded scenes, enumerate several simultaneous micro-actions instead of describing only the event category. Use a low-cost accelerated pass to test composition, but retain an explicitly labeled base-model pass when evaluating final motion and audio behavior.

## 4. Selective dancer transfer into a new environment

- **Original post and workflow explanation:** https://www.reddit.com/r/StableDiffusion/comments/1w2zz0h/minimax_h3_transferring_dancer_to_a_new/
- **Direct video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WHSRr0hy6k
- **Published:** 2026-08-31 10:50 JST
- **Author:** CQDSN
- **Theme:** transfer only the black-clothed dancer from a multi-person source video into a separately supplied environment, while ignoring the second performer in green and retaining the chosen subject’s performance.
- **Available output metadata:** final output is upscaled to 1080p; exact duration and aspect ratio were not reliably disclosed.
- **Model evidence:** the title and author explanation explicitly identify MiniMax H3 and its normal reference workflow. The final render removes the Turbo LoRA and runs 20 steps; the Turbo pass is used only for preview selection.
- **Accurate prompt/workflow summary:** Supply the source performance video and one image of the replacement environment. Identify the retained performer as “the man dressed all in black,” explicitly tell H3 to ignore everyone else, and request replacement of the background with the environment reference. Generate several 0.6-megapixel drafts with a 4-step Turbo LoRA, select the best motion, lock its seed, remove the Turbo LoRA, and rerun at 20 steps before upscaling to 1080p. The author reports that the fixed seed preserves the same broad interpretation across the draft/final setting change. For the inverse task—replacing the dancer while keeping the original environment—attach the target identity image and add side/back views when the motion exposes them.
- **Reusable pattern:** split reference-video editing into three explicit ownership rules: which person survives, which people are ignored, and which reference owns the new environment. Preserve the selected seed between a cheap preview and a full-step base render, and provide identity views for every body orientation that the source performance reveals.
