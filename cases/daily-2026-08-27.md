- **Model evidence:** the creator labels the attached result “MiniMax H3,” publishes the complete prompt in a direct reply, and the original post contains the playable generated video.
- **Accurate prompt summary:** Begin in a violent sunset sandstorm on an abandoned battlefield. Start with an extreme macro of individual grains, then let them spiral around an invisible human volume and progressively become fabric, engraved metal plates, helmet, hair, face, and katana. Move through low-angle tracking into a full 360-degree orbit as the sword is drawn. Preserve physically believable particle motion, atmospheric dust, volumetric sunset light, scale, depth of field, and transformation continuity.
- **Reusable pattern:** define a material-to-character conversion as a visible part-by-part construction order, then reuse the same material for the character’s signature attack, occlusion transition, disappearance, and reformation.

## 2. Voice-generated onomatopoeia becomes an abstract world

- **Video, original post, and full prompt:** https://x.com/su_nagomi/status/2092834537474753005
- **Published:** 2026-08-27 13:40 JST
- **Author:** すーさん / Su-san (@su_nagomi)
- **Theme:** a black silhouette speaks the syllables ク, パ, ポ, and ペ; each spoken sound becomes an exact matching Japanese character and geometric object until the created world breaks loose and collapses into “無。”
- **Available output metadata:** approximately 15.08 seconds; 1344×768; near-16:9.
- **Model evidence:** the creator explicitly states that the attached T2V video was made with MiniMax H3 and includes the complete eight-cut prompt in the same post.
- **Accurate prompt summary:** Use a warm off-white abstract void, one black silhouette, and a restrained Suprematist palette of ink black, muted red, ochre, deep blue, and gray. Across eight timecoded cuts, spoken syllables first generate individual glyph-and-shape pairs, then paths, stairs, sideways gravity, an independent shadow, and a detached speaking mouth. Accelerate into a three-voice polyrhythm inside the mouth-space; finally have the protagonist inhale every glyph, shape, shadow, and mouth. Hold 0.4 seconds of total silence and empty frame before revealing only “無。” at 14.2 seconds. Require every displayed glyph to match the currently spoken syllable exactly and forbid random text, music, ambience, fluorescent color, photoreal backgrounds, and horror-like bodily damage.
- **Reusable pattern:** when audio must control typography, define a closed symbol vocabulary, a one-to-one sound-to-glyph mapping, and a deterministic order for compound sounds. Build narrative escalation by letting the generated symbols evolve from decoration into terrain, agents, and finally the conflict itself.

Open the shop and form its sign; animate a Tokyo-to-Miyazaki route on a simplified map; grow a cardamom field; introduce the farmer; isolate one pod in close-up; show the owner biting it and reacting; transfer a bag of pods; pour curry and add three pods; assemble curry, salad, and lassi; then reveal the exact limited-menu name, price, and included items. Lock a five-color palette, thick near-black outlines, flat fills, smartphone-readable Japanese text, and no gradients, shadows, 3D, or invented copy. Pair every visual beat with matching shutter, aircraft, leaf, pod, aroma, curry, ceramic, typography, and impact sounds, plus a 15-second Japanese commercial jingle whose instrumentation and intensity follow the color chapters.

## 4. Brand-system board rebuilt as a kinetic TV commercial

- **Video and original post:** https://x.com/ponzponz15/status/2092818051075133545
- **Published H3 prompt:** https://x.com/ponzponz15/status/2092818055319654573
- **Published:** 2026-08-27 12:34 JST
- **Author:** Ponz / ぽんず (@ponzponz15)
- **Theme:** a static SHIROKUMA brand-system board becomes a rapid Japanese editorial motion-graphics commercial led by a photoreal polar-bear creative director.
- **Available output metadata:** the attached comparison video is approximately 10.18 seconds at 1080×960; the H3 generation brief explicitly targets a 10-second 16:9 film.
- **Model evidence:** the creator identifies the right-hand result as MiniMax H3, attaches the playable comparison, and publishes the structured Ref2VA prompt in a direct reply.
- **Accurate prompt summary:** Bind the supplied board separately as the authority for the bear, exact typography, logo, UI labels, service headings, news rows, props, palette, and editorial layout. Never show the board as a full screenshot. In fourteen micro-shots, rebuild its assets into a 16:9 TVCM using hard cuts, kinetic typography, tracking shifts, registration snaps, masked reveals, stamps, diagonal wipes, radial pulses, and object-driven transitions. Preserve the same bear in the required shots and permit only text copied from the board. Maintain three contrast states—dark on light, light on dark, or black/white on red—and open a temporary matte or contrast window whenever text crosses dark fur, glasses, clothing, or shadows. Let most shots run for 0.3–0.7 seconds, but hold settled type for 8–12 frames and keep the final logo, tagline, and call to action fully stable from about 9.15 seconds.
- **Reusable pattern:** treat a design-system board as a database of independently addressable assets, not as a composition to animate. Bind identity, copy, and graphic grammar as separate subjects, then convert existing marks—underlines, arrows, brackets, circles, and cards—into transitions between micro-shots.
