Treat the uploaded character as the definitive authority for face, hair, palette, proportions or species, clothing construction, accessories, and illustration style. Build a mechanically plausible sequence through small steps, support-foot changes, weight transfers, pivots, continuous arm gestures, and progressively changing orientation, height, balance, and momentum. Keep secondary hair, fabric, and accessories lagging, swinging, and settling under gravity. Forbid repeated or abrupt poses, identity or costume drift, new props or scenery, captions or watermarks, malformed or cropped anatomy, and scale or orientation jumps.
- **Reusable pattern:** use one numbered key-pose sheet as both the identity ledger and choreography ledger; make the reading order explicit, describe support and weight transfer, and lock a static full-body camera so H3 interpolates the sequence rather than cutting between panels.

## 2. An explosive escape whose apparent exit becomes the real trap

- **Video:** https://x.com/ou_zhen599/status/2100103854650179756/video/1
- **Original post and full prompt:** https://x.com/ou_zhen599/status/2100103854650179756

- **Author:** Loriel.AI (@ou_zhen599)
- **Theme:** an explorer outruns an explosion across a rain-soaked temple bridge, vaults a gap toward a lightning-lit arch, then realizes the chase has deliberately driven her onto a sinking mechanism.
- **Available output metadata:** 14.848 seconds, 480 × 270 public stream, 16:9. The prompt targets 15 seconds.
- **Model evidence:** the original post explicitly states “Made with MiniMax-H3 ComfyUI Image-to-Video,” embeds the generated video, and publishes the complete timecoded MiniMax-H3 prompt.
- **Original prompt, accurately condensed:** Lock the entire clip to the same rain-soaked bridge axis, broken arcades, mossy railings, burning ruin, and storm sky from the first frame; preserve the same adult explorer, wet hairstyle, clothing, holster, straps, mud, skin, and materials. Link camera changes through wet-hair wipes, splash occlusion, close body passes, and inertia. At 0–2 seconds retreat near her right-front shoulder as she sprints and glances back while the explosion first pushes water and mist, then rubble, sparks, mud, and ash. At 2–5.5 seconds track backward in front as each boot compresses water, weight rolls heel-to-toe, the knee absorbs impact, and the opposite foot pushes off; she steadies the holster and says, “This isn’t collapse.” At 5.5–9 seconds let lightning reveal an apparent exit; she grips the slick railing, uses arm, shoulder, and back force to sling around a broken gap, and lands with a weight-bearing knee and delayed strap and hair rebound. At 9–12.2 seconds light buried cold-gold seams ahead, sink the stone joints, and make her brake physically: traction fails, momentum carries her forward, her gaze drops, and her rear hand grabs the railing. At 12.2–15 seconds show the pullback, body rebound, probing foot, continuing subsidence, and the realization line, “It wasn’t trying to kill me. It was driving me here.” Keep rain, fire, thunder, footfalls, wet grip, falling grit, mechanism sounds, breathing, and dialogue separate; use no melody and end without black.
- **Reusable pattern:** construct a false escape as a physical causality chain—blast pressure, sprint mechanics, grip-assisted vault, lightning reveal, traction loss, railing catch, structural movement, then deduction—and let every emotional change occur only after its visible trigger.
