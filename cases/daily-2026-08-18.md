## 1. Victor M — generate playable game-sprite animations from one character still

- **Published:** 2026-08-17 20:17 JST; MiniMax official amplification on 2026-08-18 03:33 JST
- **Author:** Victor M (`@victormustar`)
- **Video and original workflow post:** https://x.com/victormustar/status/2089310616854892818
- **MiniMax official confirmation:** https://x.com/MiniMax_AI/status/2089420340728610890
- **Theme:** Turn one character design into a consistent set of playable 2D game animations.
- **Aspect ratio / duration:** Aspect ratio not stated; one 5-second H3 clip is generated per move. The demonstrated set contains eight moves.
- **Prompt/workflow availability:** The source publishes the workflow rather than a complete per-move prompt.
- **Accurate workflow summary:** Start from one full-character design on a flat magenta background. Generate a separate five-second MiniMax H3 clip for each move, such as walking or jumping. Trim each clip to the useful pose range, chroma-key the magenta, pack the extracted frames into a sprite atlas, and wire the animations to a state machine. The author reports about US$5 for eight moves.
- **Reusable prompt pattern:** Give every motion its own short clip and use the same full-body still plus an invariant high-contrast background for all clips. Describe one clean cyclic or single-purpose action per generation; perform transparency extraction and state-machine assembly outside H3.
- **H3 evidence:** The original post explicitly names “MiniMax-H3.” MiniMax’s official account reposted the same demo and described it as a MiniMax H3 game-sprite use case.

## 3. Atlas Cloud — two-shot ramen-stall continuity using image, video, and audio references

- **Published:** 2026-08-17
- **Author:** Atlas Cloud
- **Source article and full experiment:** https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/minimax-h3-reference-to-video
- **Showcase video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nT2VtaBlUYA
- **Theme:** Preserve one street-food chef, a distinctive ramen bowl, grade/grain, and an audio bed across a rainy night alley and a different morning market.
- **Aspect ratio / duration:** Two 8-second shots, each 2K (2560×1440), 16:9, 24 fps, with native 32 kHz stereo audio; combined showcase is 16 seconds.
- **Prompt availability:** Both H3 prompts, reference-image prompts, settings, measured outputs, and failure tests are published.
- **Accurate prompt summary:**
  - Create a neutral studio portrait with cheap visual identity checks: a scar above the right eyebrow, short tucked hair, indigo work jacket, and folded white towel.
  - Create a separate product reference for a navy ramen bowl with a white crane and chipped rim.
  - **Shot A:** Use both image references. Establish a narrow neon alley in heavy rain; the chef ladles broth into the referenced bowl under a plastic awning while the camera slowly pushes in. Direct rain, boiling broth, and distant traffic with no dialogue.
  - **Shot B:** Pass the character portrait, Shot A itself as a video reference, and an eight-second sparse jazz clip. Move to an empty covered market the next morning; keep the same face, scar, hair, jacket, and towel while she wipes the counter, briefly addresses the camera, and returns to work.
- **Measured finding:** The prior video reference successfully carried identity, grade, and grain, but it also overpowered the request for bright daylight. The reference audio influenced the returned mix but was not reproduced exactly.
- **Reusable pattern:** For cross-shot continuity, let a neutral image own identity, a distinctive object image own the prop, the previous shot own visual continuity, and a short audio clip steer the mix. Do not simultaneously demand the previous shot’s grade and an opposite lighting treatment; when the new light matters more, remove the grade instruction or the video reference.
- **H3 evidence:** The article names the exact endpoint `minimax/h3/reference-to-video`, publishes completed-job settings and timings, and embeds the generated two-shot result.
