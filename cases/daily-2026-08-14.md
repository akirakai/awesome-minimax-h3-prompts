## 1. Parameterized 13-cut character-introduction template

- **Published:** 2026-08-13 18:08 JST
- **Author:** Kōda (`@aimikoda`)
- **Source and video:** [Main X post](https://x.com/aimikoda/status/2087828526577705303)
- **Prompt thread:** [Published H3 template](https://x.com/aimikoda/status/2087828806178418963)
- **Theme:** Premium game-trailer-style character reveal driven by a character sheet and editable text parameters
- **Duration:** 15 seconds
- **Aspect ratio:** Not disclosed
- **H3 evidence:** The creator titles the post “MiniMax H3 Character Introduction Template”; the same thread contains the generated video, character sheet, and prompt.

### Published prompt structure — accurate summary

- Defines reusable variables such as `CHARACTER_NAME`, `SUBTITLE`, and optional `PROJECT_TEXT` instead of hard-coding each title.
- Requests exactly 13 distinct cuts inside a 15-second character-reveal trailer.
- Uses oversized condensed white or pale-grey typography and allows the character to overlap the type to create depth.
- Directs an aggressive premium game-trailer edit rhythm: hard cuts, graphic matches, whip pans, freeze frames, impact flashes, and directional transitions.
- Treats the supplied character sheet as the authority for identity and design while the text variables control the presentation layer.

### Reusable pattern

Separate a reusable H3 prompt into two layers: **editable parameters** for names/copy and a **locked directing grammar** for cut count, reveal order, type behavior, transitions, and edit rhythm. This lets the same prompt become a repeatable character-launch template.

## 2. Directional stereo-audio creature approach — paired tests

- **Published:** 2026-08-13 03:33 and 04:57 JST
- **Author:** cocktail peanut (`@cocktailpeanut`)
- **Videos and prompts:** [Busy city-street test](https://x.com/cocktailpeanut/status/2087608478080852159) · [Open rooftop/field depth test](https://x.com/cocktailpeanut/status/2087629530953547852)
- **Theme:** A huge unseen or partially revealed creature approaches through space; direction and distance are conveyed primarily through native stereo sound
- **Duration:** 15 seconds each
- **Aspect ratio:** Not disclosed
- **H3 evidence:** Both posts explicitly identify MiniMax H3, contain the generated videos, and publish the prompts in the post text.

### Published prompt structure — accurate summary

- Establish a mundane recording baseline: hyper-realistic handheld phone footage, natural hand shake, slight autofocus breathing, and no cinematic grade.
- In the city version, frame an ordinary block with parked cars, pedestrians, storefronts, and traffic before anything abnormal happens.
- Begin with normal ambient sound, then introduce a very deep impact from the far left; continue the impacts so their changing position makes the creature's movement trackable in stereo.
- The second version moves the same sound-first idea to a quiet open rooftop or empty field, giving the directional impacts more acoustic space.
- Make audio the primary effect: impacts should feel physically located, huge, and directional; omit music.

### Reusable pattern

For native-audio H3 scenes, describe a **neutral acoustic baseline**, then specify **sound source direction → distance change → repeated spatial movement → visual reaction**. Explicitly removing music prevents the score from masking the stereo-positioning test.

## 3. Cyberpunk motorcycle: timed reveal of different text on each side

- **Published:** 2026-08-14 (JST)
- **Author:** `papjak`
- **Source, video, and full prompt:** [Reddit / r-comfyui](https://www.reddit.com/r/comfyui/comments/1vmbrtn/shoutout_to_the_comfyui_team_and_devs_grateful_to/)
- **Theme:** A rider speaks to camera, performs a smoky 180-degree burnout, reveals different logos on the two motorcycle fairings, then accelerates away in a wheelie
- **Format:** MiniMax H3 Basic Workflow; 15 seconds; 0.4 MP; local generation on 24 GB VRAM
- **Aspect ratio:** Not disclosed
- **H3 evidence:** The author names the workflow “MiniMax H3 Basic Workflow,” supplies the H3 video in the post, and publishes the complete timed prompt and render settings.

### Published prompt structure — accurate summary

- **0–3s:** Extreme close-up. The helmeted rider raises the visor, looks into camera, and delivers a short line with visible lip movement.
- **3–9s:** Low angle on a red sportbike. The left fairing carries one exact text label. A stationary burnout and sharp 180-degree rotation fully occlude that side before revealing a different exact label on the right fairing.
- **9–15s:** The bike snaps out of the turn, launches into a wheelie, and disappears down the neon road while the camera stays planted.
- The author reports that adding explicit timing was the change that made the two different side labels render correctly.

### Reusable pattern

When one rigid object must show different text on opposite sides, bind each label to a named surface and schedule a **full occlusion event** between the two reveals. The rotation is not decorative; it gives H3 a temporal boundary at which the visible text is allowed to change.

## 4. 29.25-second painterly story using two chained H3 clips

- **Published:** 2026-08-14 (JST)
- **Author:** `GamerVick`
- **Video and production notes:** [Reddit / r-StableDiffusion](https://www.reddit.com/r/StableDiffusion/comments/1vnq6cp/my_first_30second_minimax_h3_story_using_two/)
- **Full prompt:** [Pastebin](https://pastebin.com/3UPLJ5V3)
- **Motion-context workflow:** [ComfyUI-MiniMaxH3-Contex-Loop](https://github.com/ethanfel/ComfyUI-MiniMaxH3-Contex-Loop)
- **Theme:** A hungry young man steals bread in a market, enters an alley, sees a starving child, turns back, and shares the loaf
- **Format:** Two chained clips of about 15 seconds; final duration 29.25 seconds
- **Resolution / ratio:** Native 1344×768 (16:9), then NVIDIA RTX upscale to 1080p
- **References:** Six approximately 2K images
- **H3 evidence:** The author explicitly lists the model as MiniMax H3, embeds the generated video, links the complete prompt, and identifies the motion-context workflow used for clip-to-clip continuation.

### Published prompt structure — accurate summary

**Shared layer**

- Lock one painterly visual language, the main character's face, hair, costume, and the lower-city environment across both clips.
- Assign six references separate jobs: character design, costume/lighting, face close-up, baker identity, alley environment/staging, and the final bread-sharing composition.
- Explicitly forbid copying character-sheet layouts, neutral poses, swatches, and prop insets into the video.
- State that unfinished body and camera motion must continue between parts without resetting positions or actions.

**Scene 1: market theft**

- Six timecoded shots take the character from walking through the market to noticing bread, pulling up his hood while screened from the baker, stealing exactly one loaf while she completes one uninterrupted tray action, and entering the alley with the loaf visibly held in the same hand.
- The prompt repeatedly names what must remain off-screen, which direction the baker faces, which hand holds the loaf, and when an action may complete.

**Scene 2: decision and sharing**

- Five timecoded shots continue the prior closing movement. The man walks past the seated child with clear lateral separation, takes three more steps, stops, looks back, retraces the same path, kneels, breaks the loaf, and reaches the supplied final composition.
- The whole loaf stays exclusively in the right hand until the final shot; only then may the left hand touch it.
- Market ambience, footsteps, cloth, bread-crust sound, and a continuous cello phrase bridge the two clips.

### Reusable pattern

For chained narrative clips, use a shared continuity header plus per-scene shot blocks. Treat **screen position, travel axis, hand/prop ownership, incomplete secondary actions, off-screen exclusions, end composition, and continuing audio** as explicit state variables. This is substantially more precise than merely telling the next clip to “continue.”
